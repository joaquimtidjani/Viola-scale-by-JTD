Fingerings

Only the fingerings printed in the Galamian book are shown (above and below the staff, as in the book). Ⓡ = remain in position; C, G, D, A = string. Notes without a number are not fingered in the book.
Source: The Galamian Scale System adapted for viola (Karen Olson, Galaxy/ECS). Included so far: two-octave scales (groups of eight, with the opening turn 1-3-2-1) and arpeggios (groups of six); broken thirds in three octaves (or two, cut from Vi- to -de); three-octave scales in groups of twelve, major, melodic and harmonic minor, with the three fingering options of the book. The other chapters are listed and will be added.

Only the fingerings printed by Carl Flesch are shown. Notes without a number are not fingered in the book.
Source: Flesch, Scale System for viola (C. Karman). Contents of the book, for each key: exercises 1 to 4, the scale, arpeggios, broken thirds and chromatic scale on one string (1st, 2nd, 3rd octave); exercise 5, the same over three octaves; exercises 6, 7, 8: thirds, sixths, octaves (here, the first bar, re-read note by note); exercises 9 to 11: fingered octaves, tenths, harmonics (not included here). When Flesch prints two fingerings, the second one is below the staff.

Score

Viola fingerboard

Only the notes played are shown, on the string where they are played. The number is the finger printed in the book (“4/3”: same note taken by two fingers); empty circle: finger not marked. ½ = fingers close together (half step), T = fingers apart (whole step). The neck is drawn as you see it when playing: scroll at the top, C string on the left; open strings sit on the nut. A white zigzag means the neck is cut (higher positions).
Tap a note (on the staff or the fingerboard) to see its name, string, finger and position.
